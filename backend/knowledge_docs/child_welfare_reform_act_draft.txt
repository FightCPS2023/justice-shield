To abolish the corrupt and failed Child Protective Services system, establish a new transparent and 
accountable Department of Child Welfare to protect children while preserving families, implement 
robust training, oversight and standards, provide comprehensive support services, ensure 
restitution for past injustices, avoid unwarranted child removals except in cases of imminent 
danger, and promote the importance of both mothers and fathers. 
Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in 
Congress assembled, 
SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE 
This Act may be cited as the "Comprehensive Child Welfare Reform and Accountability Act of 
2024" . 
SECTION 2. FINDINGS 
(1) The current Child Protective Services (CPS) system has become rampant with corruption, 
unethical conduct enabling child trafficking, and systemic failures that have resulted in over 18,000 
children going missing from custody over the past decade alone. 
(2) Since 2010, an estimated 550,000 children nationwide have been unwarrantedly removed from 
safe, loving family environments by CPS based on scant, unreliable evidence or questionable 
reasoning that would not meet evidentiary standards in court. 
(3) Investigations have revealed thousands of children were funneled from CPS custody into child 
trafficking rings through employee negligence or collusion with criminal networks. 
(4) Legal doctrines of confidentiality, qualified immunity and absolute immunity have shielded CPS 
employees from accountability and proper oversight, fostering an environment of unchecked 
misconduct. 
(5) Prioritizing family preservation through comprehensive voluntary support services is critical to 
preventing long-lasting childhood trauma resulting from unnecessary family separations. 
(6) Both mothers and fathers play equally crucial roles in providing stable, nurturing environments 
essential for children's healthy physical and emotional development, a reality undervalued by the 
current system. 
SECTION 3. ABOLITION OF FAILED CHILD PROTECTIVE SERVICES 
(a) TERMINATION OF EXISTING SYSTEM - The Child Protective Services system shall be abolished 
and permanently terminated effective 180 days after the date of enactment of this Act. 

(b) TERMINATION OF FUNDING - All federal programs, grants or other funding sources for the 
purposes of the former Child Protective Services system shall be permanently terminated on the 
same abolition date. 
(c) RETURN OF CHILDREN - Any child residing in the custody of the former CPS system as of the 
termination date shall be immediately reunited with their family of origin pending further 
investigation and determination by the newly established Department of Child Welfare, and without 
prejudice from any prior actions or accusations. 
SECTION 4. ESTABLISHMENT OF DEPARTMENT OF CHILD WELFARE 
(a) NEW EXECUTIVE DEPARTMENT - There is established as an independent establishment in the 
executive branch the Department of Child Welfare (hereinafter referred to as the "DCW"). 
(b) MISSION - The mission of the Department of Child Welfare shall be to protect the wellbeing, 
safety and rights of children while prioritizing keeping families together through prevention and 
supportive services in a fully transparent and accountable manner. 
(c) DUTIES - The duties of the Department of Child Welfare shall be as follows: 
(1) To protect children from imminent danger of physical harm or severe demonstrable trauma 
through investigation of credible evidence meeting strict standards, and emergency separation only 
as an absolute last resort when such danger is clearly present in the home environment. 
(2) To prioritize providing voluntary comprehensive support services to families to resolve any 
issues or challenges and maintain the stable family unit before considering temporary or 
permanent child removal actions. 
(3) To thoroughly investigate any allegations of abuse or neglect using only legitimately obtained 
clear and convincing evidence that meets proper evidentiary standards, and avoid relying solely on 
hearsay, unproven accusations or low standards like preponderance of evidence. 
(4) To establish clear legal definitions and objective guidelines grounded in recognized expert 
standards as to what constitutes child abuse and neglect, ensuring consistency across all 
jurisdictions. 
(5) To provide a comprehensive array of voluntary support services and preventative resources for 
struggling families including but not limited to: counseling, substance abuse treatment, domestic 
conflict resolution, mental health programs, anger management, parenting skills training, job 
placement assistance, financial literacy education, housing support, childcare, and other 
interventions as needed to empower families and prevent issues from escalating. 
(6) To promote public awareness and appreciation of the vital importance of both mothers and 
fathers as nurturing role models essential to children's upbringing and development in a stable two-
parent household whenever possible. 
(7) To maintain full accountability, oversight and transparency over all departmental operations, 
including publication of data, stringent employee conduct requirements, external monitoring, and 
public parliamentary inquiry access. 

(d) STANDARDS FOR EMERGENCY REMOVAL - The strict evidentiary standards and immediate 
danger thresholds required for emergency separation of a child from their family enumerated in 
subsection (c)(1) shall meet or exceed the "clear and convincing evidence" criteria, and require 
objectively documented proof and specific articulable factors establishing the child faces a risk of 
imminent bodily harm, sexual abuse or severe psychological trauma if not immediately removed 
from their home environment. 
(e) FAMILY REUNIFICATION MANDATE - For any child separated from their family under the 
emergency circumstances outlined in subsection (d), the Department of Child Welfare shall make 
active and continuous efforts to provide all available support services and interventions to 
remediate any issues and accomplish the safe, permanent reunification of the child with their 
family at the earliest possible opportunity. 
(f) LEGAL ACCOUNTABILITY OF EMPLOYEES - No employee, contractor or individual conducting 
any official duties, investigations or other operational functions involving the Department of Child 
Welfare shall enjoy or be granted any form of qualified immunity, civil liability immunity, 
prosecutorial immunity, or other doctrine providing legal protections against accountability for their 
actions. Any violations of laws, codes of conduct, regulations or the provisions of this Act shall 
result in full legal liability. 
(g) PUBLIC ACCESS TO RECORDS - All official records, case files, investigative documentation, 
evidence, data and other materials maintained by the Department of Child Welfare related to its 
operations shall be preserved and made available for public inspection by default, with only narrow 
exceptions to redact individually identifiable private information explicitly permitted by existing 
federal privacy statutes and regulations. 
SECTION 5. PROHIBITIONS ON UNWARRANTED CHILD REMOVALS 
(a) EVIDENTIARY STANDARDS FOR REMOVAL - A child shall not be removed or separated from their 
family home except in temporary emergency circumstances outlined in Section 4(d) where the 
Department of Child Welfare has clear, convincing and legitimately obtained evidence meeting a 
burden of proof standard that the child is in imminent danger of bodily harm, sexual abuse or 
severe psychological trauma if they remain in that home environment. No child shall be removed 
from their home solely based on hearsay, unproven allegations, or low standards such as a 
"preponderance of the evidence" . 
(b) PRESUMPTION OF FAMILY UNITY - In all cases not meeting the strict imminent danger 
evidentiary standards of subsection (a), there shall be a presumption that the child's best interests 
are to remain united and living together with their entire family, including both parents unless 
legally determined otherwise through due process. The Department of Child Welfare shall take any 
and all reasonable measures to maintain family integrity and provide supportive services under 
Section 6 to assist any struggling families in addressing challenges before considering legal options 
for separation. 
(c) RETURN OF IMPROPERLY REMOVED CHILDREN - Any child who was previously removed from 
their family home environment by any public or private agency involved in the former failed Child 
Protective Services system, where such removal did not strictly adhere to the evidentiary 

requirements specified in subsection (a) of clear and convincing proof of an imminent danger 
situation, shall be immediately reunited with their family without prejudice by DCW to prevent any 
further injustices or trauma to those children and families. 
SECTION 6. FAMILY PRESERVATION PROGRAMS 
(a) PRESERVING FAMILY INTEGRITY - In operating the new child welfare system, the overarching 
policy priority of the Department of Child Welfare shall be preserving family unity and integrity for 
children by providing proactive voluntary preventative support services to stabilize and assist 
struggling families in overcoming any issues or challenges they face. Removal of a child from their 
family shall only ever be considered as an option of absolute last resort as specified in Section 4(d). 
(b) SUPPORT SERVICE REQUIREMENTS - The Department of Child Welfare shall establish, fund and 
administer an array of comprehensive support service programs available on a fully voluntary basis 
to any family in need across all 50 states and territories. At a minimum, these services shall include 
but are not limited to: 
(1) Individual and family mental health counseling using trauma-informed approaches, 
(2) Evidence-based parenting skills education and training programs, 
(3) Domestic conflict resolution and healthy relationship programs, 
(4) Inpatient and outpatient substance abuse rehabilitation and support groups, 
(5) Anger management programs and emotional regulation training, 
(6) Job placement assistance, career counseling and vocational training services, 
(7) Financial literacy education and household financial management programs, 
(8) Temporary housing assistance and referrals to shelters or transitional homes, 
(9) Childcare providers and supervised visitation centers, 
(10) Transportation services to ensure accessibility to programs and services, 
(11) Referral networks to community organizations providing supplemental services. 
(c) PREVENTATIVE ALLOCATION OF RESOURCES - The Department of Child Welfare shall prioritize 
the allocation of its financial resources and personnel towards providing the comprehensive 
support services and family preservation programs specified in this section, with the objective of 
preventing family crises or issues from escalating to levels that could warrant considerations of 
child removal. No less than 70% of DCW's annual budgetary expenditures shall be directed to 
supporting these preventative family stabilization efforts. 
(d) VOLUNTARY PARTICIPATION - Enrollment and participation in any of the supportive services or 
programs offered under this section shall be strictly voluntary, with no punitive consequences for 
families choosing not to utilize them. Under no circumstances shall a family's voluntary non-
participation in these services alone be treated as evidence of abuse, neglect or grounds for a child 
removal consideration. 

SECTION 7. RESTITUTION FOR INJUSTICES 
(a) UNLAWFUL SEPARATION RESTITUTION - For any child returned to their family of origin under 
Section 5(c) after being improperly separated by the former CPS system without meeting the 
evidentiary standards required under this Act, the Department of Child Welfare shall provide 
monetary damages to compensate the child and their family members for the psychological and 
emotional trauma of this injustice. The amount shall be determined through an administrative 
process considering the duration of separation and resulting pain and suffering. 
(b) ENDANGERMENT RESTITUTION - In any case where DCW's internal investigations or 
independent inquiries determine that children were placed into potentially endangering situations 
through negligence or willful misconduct by former CPS employees or contractors, such as 
allowing a child to go missing from custody or enabling child trafficking schemes, the Department 
shall ensure the child and their family receive monetary restitution for this dereliction of duty. 
Amounts shall be determined based on the severity of the endangerment and trauma. 
(c) CRIMINAL CULPABILITY - In any cases where Department of Child Welfare investigations 
uncover evidence that specific employees, contractors or individuals were criminally negligent, 
complicit or participated in child trafficking operations, child endangerment or other grievous 
criminal acts in violation of federal laws, the Department shall ensure that evidence is immediately 
presented to the Department of Justice and relevant authorities to prosecute those individuals to 
the fullest extent permitted by law. 
SECTION 8. ACCOUNTABILITY MEASURES 
(a) TRAINING AND CERTIFICATION - All employees and contractors of the Department of Child 
Welfare involved in child welfare operations shall be required to complete a core training 
curriculum and obtain certification in: 
(1) Comprehensive education on all child welfare laws, policies, regulations and investigatory 
procedures at the federal and state levels, 
(2) Fundamentals of child development and trauma-responsive engagement practices, 
(3) Investigative techniques focused on properly obtaining clear and convincing evidence meeting 
strict evidentiary procedure standards, 
(4) Cultural competency to provide Services respectful of all races, ethnicities and backgrounds, 
(5) Ethics and conduct training grounded in the provisions of this Act. 
(b) CONTINUING EDUCATION - The Department shall implement continuing education 
requirements for all credentialed staff to maintain their child welfare certifications active through 
ongoing professional development courses on emerging practices, new research and other subject 
matter ensuring their skills and knowledge remain modern and effective. 
(c) OVERSIGHT AND MONITORING - To ensure full accountability and adherence to all legal and 
ethical conduct requirements by DCW, robust internal monitoring procedures shall be established, 
including: 

(1) Unannounced audits and inspections of departmental offices, facilities, documentation and 
data practices by the DCW Office of Professional Responsibility, 
(2) A comprehensive case rating and employee evaluation system directly tied to compliance with 
standards, 
(3) A hotline and formal process for reporting suspected misconduct by any DCW personnel, 
(4) Employment disciplinary policies up to termination for substantiated violations. 
(d) EXTERNAL OVERSIGHT - The Department of Child Welfare shall be subject to ongoing external 
oversight mechanisms including: 
(1) Regularly scheduled public hearings and testimony before Congress to report on operations, 
(2) Audits and investigations by the newly established Office of the Child Welfare Inspector General 
outlined in Section 40, 
(3) Reviews and inquiries by the independent Child Welfare Advisory Board established in Section 
17, 
(4) Publication of all aggregate data, reports and records as specified in Section 4(g). 
SECTION 9. RIGHTS OF CHILDREN AND PROSPECTIVE PARENTS 
(a) CHILDREN'S RIGHTS - Any child involved in a case under the supervision of the Department of 
Child Welfare, whether temporarily removed from their home or remaining with their family, shall be 
guaranteed certain enumerated rights including but not limited to: 
(1) The right to be treated with dignity, respect and fairness at all times, 
(2) The right to be free from any form of physical, sexual or emotional abuse or neglect, 
(3) The right to receive adequate food, clothing, shelter, healthcare and other basic needs, 
(4) The right to continue receiving a formal education and age-appropriate life skills training, 
(5) The right to reasonable privileged visitation and communication with their parents and other 
family members, 
(6) The right to have their voices heard and to participate in developing their own goals and 
permanency plans to the extent possible based on age and maturity, 
(7) The right to access, review and receive copies of their personal records, documents and 
information in DCW's custody upon request, 
(8) The right to a thorough, impartial and timely investigation into any allegations of violations of 
these rights or accusations of mistreatment, and 
(9) The right to make formal grievances about any perceived violations of these rights without fear of 
retaliation or punishment. 

(b) OFFICE OF THE CHILD'S RIGHTS ADVISOR - To safeguard and uphold these enumerated rights, 
the Department shall establish an Office of the Child's Rights Advisor to perform the following 
functions: 
(1) Inform children involved with DCW of their specific rights in an age-appropriate manner, 
(2) Provide legal counsel and advisement to children on their rights and options, 
(3) Act as an impartial advocate representing children's interests in legal proceedings, 
(4) Receive and thoroughly investigate any grievances related to violations of rights, and 
(5) Regularly report to Congress on the agency's adherence to upholding children's rights. 
(c) PROSPECTIVE PARENTS' NOTIFICATION - Any individual identified as a prospective legal parent 
or guardian of a child involved in an investigation or proceeding overseen by the Department of 
Child Welfare shall be provided clear written notification of their full legal rights and options for: 
(1) Participating in the investigation and court processes related to the child, 
(2) Accessing any relevant documentation or evidence related to their child, 
(3) Options for appealing any findings or legal determinations impacting custody, and 
(4) Their rights to due process and to have legal counsel appointed if unable to afford 
representation in any proceeding that could result in termination of parental rights. 
(d) PATERNITY ESTABLISHMENT - When a child involved in a DCW case may have a biological father 
that is unknown or unconfirmed, the Department shall make all reasonable efforts and utilize 
available mechanisms to establish legal paternity if possible, including genetic testing, 
administrative procedures and actively pursuing alleged fathers. Any confirmed fathers shall have 
equal custodial rights to their child pending further legal findings. 
SECTION 10. OPEN COURT PROCEEDINGS 
(a) GENERAL RULE - Court proceedings related to any child welfare cases investigated, managed or 
overseen by the Department of Child Welfare shall be presumptively open to the public, with only 
limited exceptions when a court specifically orders portions to be closed as outlined in subsection 
(b). 
(b) EXCEPTIONS - A presiding judge may order specific portions of a child welfare court proceeding 
to be temporarily closed to the general public and not part of the permanent public record only if 
the judge finds that allowing public access would: 
(1) Cause the disclosure of individually identifiable private information about a child that could be 
directly harmful to that child's psychological or physical wellbeing if released, 
(2) Require the release of highly sensitive information related to allegations of a criminal nature that 
could potentially prejudice or taint an ongoing law enforcement investigation if publicly disclosed. 

If a closure order is issued under this subsection, the judge must specifically articulate the 
justifications for closure in their order, which shall be as narrowly tailored as possible to 
accomplish the intended protections.

(a) PRIORITIZING CHILD-FAMILY CONNECTIONS - In the limited instances where temporary

(b) FOSTER CARE STANDARDS - In cases where temporary foster care placements must be utilized 
due to lack of available kin placements, DCW shall ensure such placements strictly adhere to 
comprehensive national standards established by the Department regarding: 
(1) Foster parent qualifications, home studies, background checks and certifications 
(2) Financial compensation policies for foster parents 
(3) Maximum allowable children per foster home 
(4) Mandatory safety requirements, childproofing and inspections 
(5) Prevention protocols and reporting requirements for any suspected incidents 
(6) Regular unannounced monitoring and audits of foster homes 
SECTION 12. EDUCATION AND DISABILITY ACCOMODATIONS 
(a) MONITORING EDUCATION STATUS - The Department of Child Welfare shall monitor and track 
the education status, progress and needs of all school-age children under its care or supervision. 
(b) EDUCATION SERVICES - DCW shall ensure children have access to academic support services 
including but not limited to tutoring, counseling, disability accommodations and extracurricular 
activities to facilitate their continued educational progress and personal development. 
(c) SCHOOL STABILITY - Except when transferring schools is deemed to be in the child's best 
interest, children shall remain enrolled at the same school they attended while living with their 
family when possible to minimize disruptions. Children shall have the right to fully participate in 
normalized school activities. 

(d) TRANSITION PLANNING - For school-age children in their middle and high school years, DCW 
shall provide age-appropriate vocational counseling, life skills training programs and future career 
planning assistance as part of their education services. 
SECTION 13. PORTABILITY OF SERVICES 
To ensure children do not lose access to critical services if they relocate between jurisdictions 
while under the care or supervision of DCW: 
(a) PORTABILITY - Any support services, programs, disability accommodations or benefits being 
provided to a child by the Department shall remain continuously available without disruption if the 
child relocates across any state, county or municipal boundaries within the United States. 
(b) INTER-JURISDICTIONAL COORDINATION - The Department shall establish processes and 
cooperative agreements with all state and local jurisdictions to facilitate seamless transferring of 
case files, records, services and benefits for children that relocate. 
SECTION 14. FEDERAL WORKFORCE REQUIREMENTS 
(a) FEDERAL EMPLOYEE REQUIREMENTS - All employees and contractors hired by the Department 
of Child Welfare to perform any duties related to child welfare services, investigations, removals or 
other operations shall be federal employees or contractors rather than being employed directly by 
individual states or localities. 
(b) SUPERIOR PROTECTIONS AND BENEFITS - These federal child welfare employees shall receive 
enhanced salary, benefits, legal protections, training and professional standards compared to child 
welfare roles at non-federal public agencies. 
(c) HUMAN CAPITAL STRATEGY - The Department shall develop and implement a comprehensive 
strategy to recruit, train, retain and provide promotional opportunities for an elite national 
workforce dedicated solely to federal public service in the field of child welfare. 
SECTION 15. NATIONAL CHILD WELFARE TRAINING CENTER 
(a) ESTABLISHMENT - The Department of Child Welfare shall establish a National Child Welfare 
Training Center to develop and maintain rigorous, standardized training curricula and certification 
programs for all departmental personnel. 
(b) CORE TRAINING AREAS - At a minimum, the core training curriculum for all child welfare roles 
shall include: 
(1) Comprehensive education on child welfare laws, practices, policies and procedures 
(2) Fundamentals of child development, psychology and trauma-informed engagement 
(3) Investigative best practices focused on obtaining legitimate clear and convincing evidence 
meeting evidentiary standards for court 
(4) Cultural competency to provide services respectful of all races, ethnicities and backgrounds 
while avoiding bias or discrimination 

(5) Professional ethics, conduct standards, and whistleblower protections grounded in this Act 
(6) Continuing education requirements to maintain active certification 
(c) CONTINUING EDUCATION - The National Training Center shall develop and administer 
continuing education programs on emerging practices, research and other topics to ensure all 
credentialed DCW staff maintain modern skills. 
SECTION 16. OFFICE OF THE CHILDREN'S OMBUDSMAN 
(a) ESTABLISHMENT - There is established as an independent agency the Office of the Children's 
Ombudsman to provide external oversight and representation of children's interests in regard to the 
Department of Child Welfare. 
(b) OMBUDSMAN - This Office shall be headed by the Children's Ombudsman, who shall be 
appointed by the President and confirmed by the Senate to a 5-year term. The Ombudsman shall 
have full authority to carry out the Office's duties specified in subsection (c). 
(c) DUTIES AND POWERS - The Office of the Children's Ombudsman shall have the following duties 
and powers: 
(1) To receive and investigate any grievances or complaints from children, families or the public 
regarding DCW employee conduct, policies or practices 
(2) Serve as an impartial advocate representing the rights and interests of children involved in the 
child welfare system 
(3) Conduct independent audits and systematic reviews of DCW operations, procedures and 
adherence to laws and policies 
(4) Issue public reports with findings and recommendations regarding observed deficiencies or 
areas for improvement at DCW 
(5) Full access to all DCW facilities, records, staff and children under DCW care or supervision for 
the purposes of investigations and reviews 
(6) Subpoena authority to compel testimony and evidence as needed for investigations 
SECTION 17. CHILD WELFARE ADVISORY BOARD 
(a) ESTABLISHMENT - There is established an independent Child Welfare Advisory Board to provide 
oversight and expert guidance to the Department of Child Welfare in an advisory capacity. 
(b) MEMBERSHIP - The Board shall consist of 11 members appointed by the President to serve 4-
year terms as follows: 
(1) Two subject matter experts in child psychology and childhood trauma 
(2) Two representatives from nationally recognized child advocacy organizations 
(3) Two individuals who were previously involved parties in child welfare cases as parents 
(4) One law enforcement representative with expertise in child trafficking investigations 

(5) One representative from a disability rights organization 
(6) One representative from a civil liberties organization focused on family rights 
(7) One representative from a leading domestic violence prevention organization 
(c) RESPONSIBILITIES - The Advisory Board shall have the following duties: 
(1) Review and evaluate the policies, practices and overall adherence to laws by DCW 
(2) Hold public hearings and meetings to receive feedback and recommendations 
(3) Issue formal advisory reports and recommendations to Congress and the DCW Secretary 
(4) Establish mechanisms for public complaints and grievances related to DCW operations 
(5) Advocate for the rights and interests of children and families involved in the welfare system 
SECTION 18. MISSING CHILDREN RESOURCES AND INVESTIGATIONS 
(a) MISSING CHILDREN DIVISION - The Department of Child Welfare shall establish a Missing 
Children Division solely dedicated to: 
(1) Developing and implementing comprehensive protocols to prevent children under DCW care or 
supervision from becoming missing or victims of abduction 
(2) Rapidly investigating any cases where children go missing from DCW custody or placements 
(b) REPORTING REQUIREMENTS - Any instance where a child under DCW care or supervision 
becomes absent from their approved placement for longer than 4 hours shall immediately trigger 
notification to the Missing Children Division to initiate search efforts. 
(c) CRIMINAL PENALTIES - Any individual who unlawfully abducts, detains, conceals or fails to 
return a child under the custody of DCW shall face enhanced criminal penalties beyond existing 
federal kidnapping statutes due to the child's status. 
(d) LAW ENFORCEMENT COORDINATION - The Missing Children Division shall closely coordinate 
all investigations and search operations related to missing children with the National Center for 
Missing and Exploited Children (NCMEC), Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI), United States 
Marshals Service, and other relevant federal, state and local law enforcement agencies as needed.  
SECTION 19. CHILD WELFARE COURT 
(a) ESTABLISHMENT - There is hereby established, under article III of the Constitution of the United 
States, a federal Child Welfare Court which shall have original jurisdiction over certain cases 
involving the Department of Child Welfare as outlined in this section. 
(b) CASE REFERRALS - In the event that a child welfare case originating in state courts implicates 
important federal questions under this Act, or if the Department of Child Welfare has reasonable 
belief that a state is not adhering to the laws and standards herein, the Secretary may certify and 
refer such a case for review by the Child Welfare Court. 

(c) JUDGES - The Child Welfare Court shall be staffed with an appropriate number of federal judges 
nominated by the President and confirmed by the Senate, with prior experience in child welfare 
jurisprudence and related areas of law. 
(d) RULES AND PROCEDURES - The Court shall establish and publish its own rules and procedures 
for conducting proceedings in a manner consistent with guaranteeing due process of law and 
adherence to this Act. 
(e) APPEALS - Final decisions by the Child Welfare Court may be appealed directly to the United 
States Supreme Court under its discretionary review. 
SECTION 20. FEDERAL ADOPTION POLICIES 
(a) ADOPTION AS LAST RESORT - The permanent termination of parental rights and adoption of a 
minor child shall only be permitted by the Department of Child Welfare after all other reasonable 
efforts at providing comprehensive support services to preserve and reunify the family have been 
attempted and objectively proven unsuccessful over an adequate period of time. 
(b) INFORMED CONSENT - Prior to any adoption proceeding, DCW shall obtain signed 
documentation from all living legal parents or guardians demonstrating they have been fully 
informed of their rights and available options for appealing or contesting the proposed adoption. 
(c) PREVENTION SERVICES - The Department shall establish adoption prevention services and 
programs aimed at providing reasonable accommodations and resources to assist at-risk families 
in stabilizing circumstances to avoid unnecessary adoptions from occurring. 
(d) RESTRICTIONS ON FEDERAL SUBSIDIES - To eliminate any perceived financial incentives for 
increasing adoption over family preservation, no federal subsidies, per-child payments or other 
funds shall be provided to any public or private entity based on the number of children adopted 
through DCW. Reimbursements shall be limited only to legitimate expenses directly related to 
processing finalized adoptions after termination of parental rights. 
SECTION 21. NATIVE AMERICAN CONSIDERATIONS 
(a) TRIBAL SOVEREIGNTY - In carrying out all responsibilities and duties under this Act, the 
Department of Child Welfare shall fully respect the sovereign rights of federally recognized Native 
American tribes and follow all applicable procedures as established in the Indian Child Welfare Act 
of 1978. 
(b) TRIBAL COORDINATION - For any cases involving Native American children, DCW shall 
coordinate and collaborate with the relevant tribe's judicial system, social services, and designated 
tribal authorities throughout the process. 
(c) NATIVE PLACEMENTS - In cases requiring emergency removal of a Native child from their family, 
the Department shall make diligent efforts to prioritize placement with other members of the child's 
extended family or within their tribal community whenever possible. 
(d) CULTURAL SERVICES - The Department shall ensure the availability of culturally competent 
support services and resources tailored for the unique traditions and heritage of Native American 
families involved in the child welfare system. 

SECTION 22. RELIGIOUS ACCOMMODATIONS 
(a) RESPECTING BELIEFS - In all investigations, removals, placements, services and other child 
welfare activities covered under this Act, the Department of Child Welfare shall make reasonable 
accommodations and considerations to respect the religious beliefs, practices and moral 
convictions of all children and families involved to the extent legally permissible. 
(b) NON-DISCRIMINATION - No child or family shall be favored or disfavored by the Department in 
any determination or action based on their professed religious faith or lack thereof. DCW 
employees are prohibited from discriminating against or unduly imposing any particular set of 
religious values. 
(c) FAITH-BASED OPTIONS - When temporary out-of-home placements are required, the 
Department shall make reasonable efforts to provide options for placement with foster families 
holding the same religious beliefs as the child's family of origin whenever available and appropriate 
based on the specific faith. 
SECTION 23. EMPLOYEE CONDUCT REQUIREMENTS 
(a) PROFESSIONAL INTEGRITY - All Department of Child Welfare personnel, whether employed 
through the federal competitive service or contracted providers, shall be regarded as designated 
professional child welfare workers carrying out duties protecting society's most vulnerable. As 
such, the highest standards of ethical conduct and integrity must be maintained. 
(b) PROHIBITED CONDUCT - While acting under any authority granted through this Act, no DCW 
officer or employee shall engage in discrimination, harassment, coercion, or any actions enabling 
or assisting criminal enterprises related to family disruption or child trafficking. 
(c) DOCUMENTATION AND DATA INTEGRITY - All investigative procedures, handling of evidence, 
official documentation, recording of information into DCW systems and data management shall be 
carried out by employees with full integrity to ensure accuracy, completeness and transparency. 
(d) DUTY TO REPORT - DCW employees, contractors, or any individuals performing services related 
to this Act shall have an affirmative legal duty to immediately report any knowledge or reasonable 
suspicion of violations, misconduct, abuse of authority, or crimes by other departmental personnel 
to the Office of the Inspector General and Department of Justice. 
SECTION 24. COORDINATION WITH OTHER AGENCIES 
The Department of Child Welfare shall coordinate and establish cooperative agreements as needed 
with other relevant federal agencies, state/local/tribal entities, law enforcement, and non-profit 
service providers for the purposes of: 
(a) Conducting joint investigations and operations to dismantle illegal child trafficking networks, 
recover victims, and fully prosecute perpetrators of the law. 
(b) Facilitating the delivery of comprehensive wrap-around services to children and families through 
existing federal programs in areas such as mental health, housing, job training, disability support, 
and substance abuse. 

(c) Ensuring compliance with all relevant federal statutes, civil rights laws, privacy regulations and 
other binding legal requirements related to investigative procedures, child removals, information 
sharing and protecting individual liberties. 
SECTION 25. REPORTS TO CONGRESS 
(a) ANNUAL PERFORMANCE REPORT - The Secretary of the Department of Child Welfare shall 
submit an annual report to Congress containing: 
(1) National statistical data on child removals, family preservation efforts, and outcomes 
(2) Analysis of the effectiveness and utilization of the support services under Section 6 
(3) Information on employee training levels and overall departmental compliance 
(4) External oversight findings from audits and program evaluations 
(5) Legislative recommendations and requests for additional resources if needed 
(b) INCIDENT REPORTING - The Secretary shall immediately report to Congress, the Office of 
Inspector General, and the Department of Justice any substantiated incidents or allegations of 
criminal activity, gross misconduct, abuse, or corruption by any departmental employees related to 
the execution of this Act. 
SECTION 26. WHISTLEBLOWER PROTECTIONS 
(a) PROHIBITION ON RETALIATION - No person employed by or contracted with the federal 
government in relation to carrying out duties under this Act may be subject to reprisal, fired, 
demoted or discriminated against as a result of disclosing information that the person reasonably 
believes is evidence of: 
(1) A violation of any law, rule or regulation; 
(2) Gross mismanagement, waste of funds or abuse of authority; 
(3) Specific danger or risk to public health or safety; or 
(4) Corruption, graft or criminal activity. 
(b) REPORTING PROCEDURES - The Department shall prescribe regulations establishing 
procedures and channels for accepting whistleblower complaints regarding the conduct of DCW 
employees, and ensuring prompt investigation of such complaints by the Inspector General or 
other appropriate authority. Relief shall be provided to complainants found to have suffered 
retaliation. 
(c) PROHIBITION ON DISCLOSURE - It shall be a violation of federal law to knowingly disclose or 
reveal the identity of any whistleblower making protected disclosures under this section without 
the expressed written consent of that person, except in cases where an official investigation 
determined the whistleblower knowingly made false allegations. 
SECTION 27. RESEARCH AND EVALUATION 

(a) RESEARCH ACTIVITIES - The Department of Child Welfare shall maintain an internal research 
division focused on rigorous study and analysis to: 
(1) Evaluate the outcomes and effectiveness of various child welfare program models, 
(2) Identify evidence-based best practices for investigations, prevention and interventions, 
(3) Examine the impacts of child abuse/neglect, trauma, family structures and other factors, and 
(4) Develop and validate new screening tools, investigative methods and service approaches. 
(b) SCIENTIFIC INTEGRITY - All research activities undertaken by DCW shall be conducted in a 
manner upholding the highest ethical principles of scientific integrity, objectivity and without bias 
or conflicts of interest. 
(c) PROGRAM EVALUATIONS - The Department shall provide for objective, rigorous third-party 
evaluations of the effectiveness and outcomes of all major DCW programs and operations on an 
ongoing basis 
SECTION 28. EMERGENCY RESPONSE TEAMS 
(a) ESTABLISHMENT - The Secretary shall establish mobile Emergency Child Welfare Response 
Teams that can be rapidly deployed to any location to respond to crisis situations involving child 
endangerment. 
(b) CAPABILITIES - These Response Teams shall be staffed with medical personnel, trauma 
response specialists, social workers, and other professionals equipped to provide: 
(1) Emergency medical evaluation, stabilization and treatment for children 
(2) Trauma counseling and psychosocial first aid services 
(3) Coordination of temporary emergency placements and children's relocation 
(4) Initial evidence collection, documentation and investigative actions 
(5) Security, law enforcement coordination, and protective services 
(c) INTERAGENCY COLLABORATION - The Emergency Response Teams shall coordinate their 
operations with other relevant federal agencies such as the Department of Homeland Security, 
Department of Health and Human Services, Department of Justice, and the National Center for 
Missing and Exploited Children as required. Teams shall also cooperate with state/local partners. 
SECTION 29. CHILD WELFARE TECHNOLOGY 
(a) INFORMATION TECHNOLOGY SYSTEMS - The Department of Child Welfare shall design, 
implement and maintain modern information technology systems to enable data management, 
case management, evidence storage, analytics and other functions critical to executing its mission. 
These systems shall have strict security protocols. 
(b) NATIONAL DATABASE - The Department shall establish a national database containing 
comprehensive records on all child welfare cases, evidence, custody determinations and other 

relevant data from all state child welfare systems. Appropriate privacy safeguards shall be 
enforced. 
(c) EVIDENCE MANAGEMENT - DCW shall utilize advanced digital evidence management 
technologies to properly obtain, catalog, secure and analyze all evidence, documentation and 
records related to investigations in a manner ensuring legitimacy and admissibility. 
(d) EMERGING TECHNOLOGIES - The Department is authorized to explore, pilot and strategically 
implement emerging technology tools that could enhance prevention, investigations, safety or 
service delivery such as: 
(1) Predictive analytics to assess risk factors and identify at-risk situations 
(2) Remote monitoring systems to track child welfare and well-being 
(3) Biometric recognition, genetic analysis and other forensic technologies 
(4) Telehealth and secure video-conferencing capabilities 
(5) Mobile apps for support services, reporting and information sharing 
SECTION 30. CHILD WELFARE GRANT PROGRAMS 
(a) RESEARCH GRANTS - The Secretary, in consultation with experts and stakeholder organizations, 
shall establish a competitive grant program to fund meritorious research projects by public, non-
profit or educational entities related to: 
(1) Causes, prevention and deterrence of child abuse and neglect 
(2) Short and long-term impacts of trauma, family disruption, and child welfare policies 
(3) Effectiveness of various interventions, program models and service approaches 
(4) Other areas that could lead to improvements in the child welfare system 
(b) SERVICE DELIVERY GRANTS - The Secretary shall make grants available to public, non-profit or 
faith-based organizations to develop, implement and rigorously evaluate promising new program 
models for delivering child welfare services such as prevention, trauma treatment, family 
reunification and related community supports. 
(c) TRAINING GRANTS - Grants may be provided to accredited institutions of higher education and 
non-profit professional associations to develop and offer interdisciplinary training programs, 
certifications and continuing education for the child welfare workforce. 
(d) DATA COLLECTION GRANTS - The Secretary may award grants to state, local, tribal or non-profit 
entities to support collecting and sharing administrative data related to child abuse and neglect, as 
well as developing best practices for reliable national data on these issues. 
SECTION 31. PUBLIC OUTREACH AND COMMUNITY ENGAGEMENT 
(a) OUTREACH REQUIREMENTS - The Department of Child Welfare shall design and implement 
public outreach campaigns at the national, state and local levels to: 

(1) Educate the general public on recognizing signs of child abuse or neglect and how to properly 
report concerns to authorities 
(2) Destigmatize seeking preventative support services for struggling families before crises arise 
(3) Promote the importance of both mothers and fathers as equally vital to children's upbringing 
(4) Disseminate information on resources available for parents and families through DCW 
(b) ENGAGEMENT STRATEGIES - These campaigns shall utilize multimedia tactics including public 
service announcements, targeted print/mail materials, school programs, faith-based partnerships, 
and coordinated engagement with stakeholder advocacy groups. 
(c) COMMUNITY PATHWAYS - The Department shall establish easily accessible community 
pathways, such as neighborhood outreach centers and dedicated hotlines, enabling families to 
seek information and voluntarily access the support services outlined in Section 6. 
SECTION 32. CRIMINAL BACKGROUND CHECKS 
(a) REQUIREMENT FOR EMPLOYEES - As a condition of employment or receiving a contract, all 
individuals being hired for staff positions at the Department of Child Welfare involved in child 
welfare operations must undergo a comprehensive criminal background check covering: 
(1) A fingerprint-based search of national crime databases at the Federal Bureau of Investigation 
(2) A search of any criminal records in each state the individual has resided 
(3) A search of any child abuse and neglect registries in each state the individual has resided 
(b) UPDATED SCREENINGS - These background checks for all DCW personnel shall be updated and 
renewed every 5 years while employed or contracted with the Department. 
(c) PROHIBITIONS FOR HIRING - No individual shall be hired, employed or permitted to work for 
DCW if their background check surfaces any of the following that could impact child welfare duties: 
(1) Conviction for a felony crime of violence, sexual offense, or crimes against children 
(2) Entering of a guilty plea or deferred adjudication for such crimes 
(3) Any substantiated report of child abuse or severe neglect listed on a state registry 
(4) Previously held a position in any capacity for the former Child Protective Services 
(d) CONTRACTS AND PARTNERS - This section shall also apply to non-profit community partners 
and contractors engaged with DCW to provide services under this Act. Background checks are 
required for their relevant personnel. 
SECTION 33. ADVISORY COMMITTEE ON CHILD WELFARE PRACTICE 
(a) ESTABLISHMENT - The Secretary shall establish an Advisory Committee on Child Welfare 
Practice to provide expert recommendations regarding the programs and operations of the 
Department of Child Welfare. 

(b) MEMBERSHIP - The Advisory Committee shall consist of 17 members appointed by the 
Secretary as follows: 
(1) 7 individuals representing nationally recognized child welfare advocacy organizations 
(2) 4 medical and mental health professionals with relevant expertise 
(3) 3 social work academics from accredited programs in the field 
(4) 2 former foster youth who were previously in the child welfare system 
(5) 1 former judge with experience presiding over child welfare cases 
(c) DUTIES - The Advisory Committee shall: 
(1) Review and analyze current DCW policies, practices, regulations and programs 
(2) Recommend modifications to improve delivery of services and outcomes 
(3) Provide technical support in developing federal standards and best practices 
(4) Submit public reports to the Secretary and Congress at least annually 
(d) COORDINATION WITH BOARDS - The Advisory Committee shall coordinate and share 
information as needed with the Child Welfare Advisory Board and other relevant federal advisory 
bodies impacting child welfare. 
SECTION 34. AUTHORIZATION OF APPROPRIATIONS 
There are authorized to be appropriated to the Department of Child Welfare such sums as may be 
necessary to properly execute all responsibilities and activities outlined in this Act.  Funding shall 
reflect the Department's overarching policy prioritization of providing robust preventative services 
to preserve family unity wherever possible. 
SECTION 35. EFFECTIVE DATE 
Except where specifically provided otherwise, this Act and the amendments made by this Act shall 
take effect 180 days after the date of enactment. The Secretary may issue guidance and 
promulgate regulations required to facilitate an orderly implementation period.

SECTION 37. POTECTION OF REASONABLE DISCIPLINE 
(a) DEFINITION OF CHILD ABUSE - For purposes of this Act, the term "child abuse" shall not include 
reasonable corporal discipline by a parent or legal guardian against a child, including spanking, as 
long as such discipline does not cause substantial bodily harm. 

(b) PARENTAL RIGHTS - Nothing in this Act shall be construed to prohibit parents or legal guardians 
from reasonably disciplining their children through non-abusive corporal punishment or from 
exercising other traditional parental rights and responsibilities regarding their children's care, 
upbringing and education. 
(c) LIMITATIONS - The Department of Child Welfare investigations and removal actions shall 
prioritize situations involving substantiated risks of severe abuse, sexual exploitation, human 
trafficking or demonstrable trauma to the child that could cause long-term damage. Interventions 
into reasonable parental discipline shall be limited. 
SECTION 38. RESTRICTIONS ON ADOPTION FROM FOSTER CARE 
(a) PREREQUISITES - In order for the Department of Child Welfare to approve adoption proceedings 
for a child from foster care: 
(1) Parental rights must first be properly and legally terminated by a court; 
(2) The child must have been in the legal custody of DCW for a minimum of 12 months; 
(3) DCW must certify that all reasonable efforts at providing support services to maintain the 
original family unit were exhausted over that period of time. 
(b) CONSENT REQUIREMENTS - Prior to adoption, the Department shall: 
(1) Make reasonable efforts to locate and notify all living biological parents; 
(2) Obtain signed documentation from those parents acknowledging they understand their legal 
rights and options to contest the adoption; 
(3) Provide a court-appointed advocate for the child during adoption proceedings. 
(c) CASE REVIEW - For each potential adoption from foster care, DCW shall conduct a 
comprehensive review examining: 
(1) The circumstances and potential for avoiding the initial removal from the home; 
(2) Support services provided and whether they were reasonably accessible; 
(3) Any concerns about the removal, Family Court process or termination of rights; 
(4) Overall consideration of the child's best interests in this specific case. 
SECTION 39. CHILD WELFARE PERSONNEL CODE OF CONDUCT 
(a) PROMULGATION - Within 180 days of enactment, the Secretary shall promulgate and put into 
effect a Code of Conduct for all Department of Child Welfare employees and contractors involved 
in executing this Act. 
(b) ETHICS REQUIREMENTS - At a minimum, the Code shall establish clear ethics rules and 
prohibited conduct including: 
(1) Prohibitions on discrimination or harassment based on protected characteristics; 

(2) Restrictions on acceptance of gifts, bribes or financial conflicts of interest; 
(3) Duty to report violations of laws, abuse of authority or child endangerment; 
(4) Confidentiality requirements and proper handling of private information; 
(5) Political activity restrictions while performing official child welfare duties; 
(6) Disciplinary penalties for substantiated ethics violations up to termination. 
(c) TRAINING - All DCW personnel shall undergo mandatory training on the Code of Conduct and 
ethical standards as a condition of employment or contract. Refresher training shall be required 
biannually. 
(d) ADHERENCE AND REPORTING - Each employee shall provide written affirmation that they have 
reviewed, understand and will adhere to the Code of Conduct. Employees have a duty to report any 
violations. 
SECTION 40. ANTI-HUMAN TRAFFICKING PROGRAM 
(a) ESTABLISHMENT - Within the Department of Child Welfare, there shall be established an Anti-
Human Trafficking Program to coordinate all efforts, investigations and operations related to 
combating child trafficking. 
(b) PROGRAM DUTIES - The Anti-Human Trafficking Program shall be responsible for: 
(1) Developing training curricula and procedures for identifying trafficking victims; 
(2) Establishing screening protocols at ports of entry, DCW facilities and partner sites; 
(3) Conducting proactive investigations into suspected trafficking operations; 
(4) Coordinating with law enforcement regarding information sharing and prosecutions; 
(5) Providing comprehensive victim support services to rescued minors; 
(6) Maintaining data on prevalence, trends and characteristics of child trafficking. 
(c) INTERAGENCY COOPERATION - The Anti-Human Trafficking Program shall establish cooperative 
arrangements and information sharing with agencies like the Department of Homeland Security, 
Department of Justice, Department of State and international partners combating child trafficking. 
SECTION 41. SUPERCEDING PROVISIONS 
The provisions contained within this Comprehensive Child Welfare Reform and Accountability Act 
shall apply and take precedence over any other previously existing federal laws, statutes or legal 
precedents that may conflict with the principles, standards and authorities outlined in this 
legislation. 
SECTION 42. GAO REPORTS AND ONGOING EVALUATIONS 
(a) INITIAL IMPLEMENTATION REVIEW - Within 2 years of the Department of Child Welfare being 
established and this Act entering into force, the Comptroller General shall conduct a 

comprehensive review and analysis of DCW's implementation of this Act's requirements and 
submit a report to Congress. 
(b) ONGOING OVERSIGHT - Following the initial review, the Government Accountability Office shall 
include within its mandated oversight plan periodic audits and evaluations of the Department of 
Child Welfare's operations, expenditures, management and outcomes to ensure continued 
legislative intent and identify any deficiencies requiring legislative corrections. 
SECTION 43. REGULATIONS 
The Secretary of the Department of Child Welfare shall promulgate all rules, regulations, policies 
and procedures required by federal law necessary to implement the provisions of this Act within 1 
year from enactment. 
SECTION 44. AMENDMENT TO TITLE 5, UNITED STATES CODE 
Title 5 of the United States Code is amended by inserting after the section enumerating the 
Department of Labor the following: 
"Department of Child Welfare 
There shall be at the seat of the Government a Department of Child Welfare. The Department shall 
be administered by the Secretary of Child Welfare, who shall be appointed by the President, by and 
with the advice and consent of the Senate. "

any person or circumstance, is held to be unconstitutional, the remainder of this Act and the 
amendments made by this Act, and the application of the provisions to any other person or 
circumstance shall not be affected. 
SECTION 46. EFFECTIVE DATE 
Except as otherwise provided in this Act, the provisions of this Act shall take effect 180 days after 
the date of enactment.
